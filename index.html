    How this email subject line score checker evaluates your copy

    Instead of grading every campaign with a generic formula, InboxBench adjusts expectations based on whether you are sending a 1-to-1 cold email, a weekly newsletter, or a time-sensitive store promotion.

    01 · 25 pts

    Mobile inbox fit

    More than half of US email opens happen on phones. Keeping key words inside the first 38 characters prevents your hook from getting cut off mid-thought.

    02 · 25 pts

    Spam & formatting hygiene

    Flags high-risk phrases ("100% free", "act now", "risk-free"), stacked exclamation marks, and all-caps shouting that push campaigns into Spam or Promotions.

    03 · 25 pts

    Specificity & readability

    Concrete numbers, plain-spoken verbs, and clear topics beat vague openers like "Checking in" or "Monthly Update" that give readers zero reason to open.

    04 · 25 pts

    Curiosity vs. false urgency

    Measures whether your line creates genuine interest without sounding manipulative. Cold outreach favors peer tone; retail promos reward crisp, honest deadlines.

    Practical subject line benchmarks by campaign type

    Use these baseline targets when writing subject lines for US audiences across B2B sales, creator newsletters, and direct-to-consumer e-commerce.

    Campaign type Target length Tone & formatting Common mistake to avoid
    B2B Cold Outreach 20–42 chars (2–6 words) Sentence case or lowercase, no emojis, reads like an internal colleague note. Sounding like a marketing flyer ("FREE demo!", "Boost ROI 10x").
    Creator & Editorial Newsletter 30–52 chars (5–8 words) Specific takeaway, contrarian stat, or focused question. Labeling only the date or issue number ("May Newsletter #14").
    Flash Sale / E-Commerce Promo 28–48 chars (4–8 words) One clear offer plus an honest timeframe ("20% off ends at midnight"). Stacking multiple urgency triggers ("URGENT! Final hours! Act now!!").
    Abandoned Cart Recovery 24–46 chars (4–7 words) Helpful reminder naming the item or category, low-pressure tone. Guilt-tripping or fake order-confirmation phrasing ("Your order status").

    Frequently asked questions

    Straight answers on subject line testing, deliverability signals, and mobile inbox previews.

    How does this free email subject line score checker calculate scores?
    The tool scores your subject line out of 100 across four weighted criteria: mobile length and word count (25 points), spam-trigger and formatting hygiene (25 points), clarity and specificity (25 points), and tone balance for your chosen campaign type (25 points).
    What is the ideal email subject line length for US mobile inboxes?
    Aim for 30 to 45 characters (about 4 to 7 words). iPhone Mail in portrait mode typically clips subject lines around 36 to 38 characters, while Gmail on Android shows roughly 40 to 45 characters before adding an ellipsis.
    Why do B2B cold emails score differently from e-commerce promos?
    B2B prospects expect a cold email subject line to look like a quick note from a peer, so short lowercase phrases without emojis or hype tend to perform best. E-commerce promos can include clear dollar or percentage savings, provided they avoid stacking multiple aggressive trigger phrases.
    Does avoiding spam-trigger words guarantee primary inbox placement?
    No single subject line check guarantees primary tab delivery. Inbox providers also weigh sender authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), list engagement, complaint rates, and email body quality. Keeping your subject line clean simply removes an avoidable filter signal.
    Is my subject line sent to an external server or stored?
    No. All scoring, spam scanning, mobile previews, and rewrite suggestions run locally inside your browser. Your drafts stay on your device.
    What should I put in the preheader text alongside my subject line?
    Treat the preheader as the second half of your subject line. Avoid repeating the subject line or leaving default text like "View in browser." Instead, add a concrete detail, timeframe, or proof point that reinforces why the email is worth opening.
